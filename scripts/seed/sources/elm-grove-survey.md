# Heat loss survey: 14 Elm Grove, Leeds

Surveyor: Gareth Owen, MCS-certified heat pump designer. Survey date 4 September.

## The house

A 1934 semi-detached house with three bedrooms, about 104 square metres of floor area over two storeys. Cavity walls were filled in 2011. The loft has 200 mm of mineral wool. Windows are double glazed throughout, fitted around 2008, except the stained-glass panel beside the front door. The ground floor is suspended timber over a ventilated void, with no insulation.

## Design conditions

Design outdoor temperature for Leeds: minus 2.2 °C. Design indoor temperatures: 21 °C in the living rooms and bathroom, 18 °C in the bedrooms and hall.

## Heat loss

Room-by-room calculation to BS EN 12831 gives a **total design heat loss of 8.4 kW**. The largest single loss is through the uninsulated suspended floor (1.6 kW), followed by ventilation in the through-lounge (1.3 kW).

| Room | Heat loss (W) | Existing radiator output at 50 °C flow (W) | Shortfall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Through-lounge | 2,310 | 1,480 | Yes |
| Kitchen-diner | 1,640 | 1,720 | No |
| Hall and landing | 1,050 | 610 | Yes |
| Bedroom 1 | 1,180 | 990 | Yes |
| Bedroom 2 | 980 | 1,010 | No |
| Bedroom 3 | 560 | 640 | No |
| Bathroom | 680 | 420 | Yes |

## Recommendations

1. An air-source heat pump of **9 kW nominal output** at the design condition. A 7 kW unit would fall short on the coldest days; a 12 kW unit would cycle too often in spring and autumn.
2. Replace four radiators (lounge, hall, bedroom 1, bathroom) with larger double-panel convectors sized for a 50 °C flow temperature.
3. Insulate under the suspended ground floor before or alongside the install. This would cut the design heat loss by about 1.2 kW and might allow the lounge radiator to stay.
4. A 200-litre unvented cylinder in the airing cupboard, replacing the combination boiler's instantaneous hot water.

## Siting

The outdoor unit can sit on the side return, 1.8 m from the boundary with number 12. The permitted development noise limit (42 dB at the neighbour's nearest window) is met by the 9 kW units we quote, with a margin of 3 to 5 dB.
